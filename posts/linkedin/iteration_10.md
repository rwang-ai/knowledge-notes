Learning in Public Series #10

When MVP Finally Clicked

After working at pre-seed to Series A startups, something Tenni taught in BellAI finally landed for me.
For most of my career, I didn’t distinguish MVP from POC.
At large, established companies, you rarely feel pressure to test cheaply. My work was usually about finding the fastest path to confirm feasibility so execution stayed predictable.
Early-stage environments made me realize I needed a different definition of “progress.”

The Mental Model
A POC proves something is technically possible.
An MVP tests whether a hypothesis is true — with the smallest, cheapest experiment that produces real signal.
Once you anchor around the hypothesis, prioritization becomes clear:
The question shifts from “What features matter?” to “What do we need to learn next?”
And each hypothesis needs grounded measurement, not just vibes.
Even early feedback from 10–20 users — friends, internal users, early adopters — can overturn assumptions quickly. We all get attached to our own ideas; measurement keeps that in check.

Build → Measure → Learn (Practically)
Across the entire cycle, Claude Code has become a surprisingly handy data-analysis partner. It’s direct, non-flattering, and doesn’t bluff. When I feed it early numbers or engagement data, it calls out issues plainly and helps brainstorm solutions and identify what to test next.

The Breakthrough
Simple concept, but it changed how I work:
- Iteration speed comes from clarity.
- Clarity comes from hypotheses.
- And hypotheses only matter if you measure them.

Learning to cut everything down to the essentials has been one of my biggest shifts this year — and it’s helping me move faster with more confidence in 0→1 environments.
