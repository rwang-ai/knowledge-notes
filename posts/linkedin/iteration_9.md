Learning in Public Series #9
I’ve been trying to put into words my intuition on how to collaborate with AI. What helped was thinking about the different phases of learning—and realizing that what we delegate to AI depends on where we are in the cycle.

If I categorize the different states of learning, they look like this:

- Activation – getting exposed, primed, curious
- Learning – digesting, connecting, practicing
- Struggling – the deliberate effort that strengthens memory and skills
- Output – producing results
- Reflection – consolidating and improving

Here’s how I think about AI’s role—and the phases where it’s helpful versus where I keep it aside:
- Activation: AI dramatically lowers the cost of starting. It helps you explore, experiment, and get early feedback quickly, letting you feel a sense of accomplishment early and building momentum for further learning.
- Learning: This is where deliberate structure matters most. Using frameworks, guided exercises, or step-by-step practice helps concepts stick. Slow down your processing—don’t mistake AI slop for knowledge that’s truly understood and retained.
- Struggling: This phase is essential—like a workout session for your brain, building mental “muscles.” AI can offer hints, alternative perspectives, or prompts, but the effort and friction should remain yours.
- Output: This is the stage where knowledge is applied to create value, and structure amplifies AI’s strengths. Clear plans, test-first approaches, and review scaffolds help turn AI-generated ideas into reliable, production-ready results.
- Reflection: At this stage, AI can help you synthesize learning, refine processes, and organize insights for future action.

Takeaway: Not every stage of learning benefits equally from AI. Being aware of where you are in the cycle helps you use it deliberately and avoid over-reliance, letting AI amplify your learning rather than replace it.
